How we tested

  1. Freeze. The AI takes the side that lost and sees only the brief and {otherFiles} other documents from the docket ({docket}), all filed by {filed}. The other side’s papers and the court’s order are held out.
  2. Map. A model turns the brief into {claims} claims, {steps} reasoning steps and {premises} points it relies on, {unstated} of them never stated.
  3. Check. Each stated point is searched for in the record, and each case citation is looked up on CourtListener: {anchored} of {stated} are backed by a quote or a checked case.
  4. Attack. Three AI attackers, for case law, the record and the argument’s structure, produce {attacks} attacks. Every quote must be found in the filings and every cited case decided before filing day.
  5. Answer. An AI defense answers each attack, AI opposing counsel counters, and an AI judge calls it answered, disputed or still open.
  6. Read. A frontier model calls each claim three times: from the brief alone, with the attacks, and with the AI judge’s rulings too.
  7. Score. Only now is the order opened. We count {reasons} of its {orderPoints} points as deciding, and match each by hand to the first attack in the list that makes the same point.

One case and one run, matched by hand. Asked about the case with no documents, the model did not recall it.
