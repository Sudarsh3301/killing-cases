DEFENDANT’S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT 
 
 
 
Pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56, and this Court’s Local Rules, 
the Defendant herein, K-VA-T Food Stores, Inc, d/b/a Food City (“Food City”) 
hereby MOVES this Honorable Court for summary judgment as to all of the 
allegations in the Complaint.  
Food City files herewith the Statement of Material Facts as to which there is 
no genuine issue, the supporting Memorandum of Law and the following exhibits:  
Exhibit 1: Deposition of Plaintiff Batten (Ex. 1, Pl.’s Depo.).  
Exhibit 2: Deposition of Gary Richards, (Ex. 2, Richards Depo). 
Exhibit 3: Deposition of David Ellison, (Ex. 3, Ellison Depo).  
Exhibit 4: Deposition of Sherrill Lyons, (Ex. 4 Lyon Depo).  
Exhibit 5: Deposition of Donnie Meadows, (Ex.5, Meadows Depo).

Plaintiff alleges Food City violated the Americans with Disabilities Act of 
1990 and the ADAAA of 2008 (collectively, “ADA”) by failing to provide him with 
reasonable accommodations and failing to engage in the interactive process. Plaintiff 
also alleges Food City discriminated against him and discharged him because of a 
disability, or his record of having a disability or because Food City regarded him as 
having a disability in violation of the ADA. Lastly, Plaintiff alleges Food City 
retaliated against him for engaging in a protected activity in violation of the ADA.   
Food City did not discriminate against Plaintiff on the basis of his disability 
or on any other basis prohibited by law. Food City did not fail to provide a reasonable 
accommodation to the Plaintiff. The undisputed material facts show that Food City 
in fact initiated the interactive process with Plaintiff in an effort to gather information 
and discuss potential alternative accommodations to Plaintiff but Plaintiff advised 
Food City he would not accept any accommodation except being allowed to bring 
his dog to work.  
The undisputed material facts show that Plaintiff did not require a service 
animal to perform the essential functions of his job and that to the extent he sought 
to bring his service animal to address stability concerns, there were alternative 
reasonable accommodations which Plaintiff made clear to Food City that he would 
not consider. The undisputed material facts show that Plaintiff failed to engage in

the interactive process with Food City to identify an accommodation because he 
stated he would not work without his service animal.     
The undisputed material facts show that Food City did not terminate Plaintiff 
in retaliation for engaging in a protected activity under the ADA. There is no 
evidence of retaliation on the part of Food City. In addition, the undisputed material 
facts establish that there is no causal link between protected activity on the part of 
Plaintiff and his separation from employment.   
Based on the applicable law, and pursuant to the Americans with Disabilities 
Act of 1990 and the Americans with Disabilities Amendments Act of 2008, the 
Defendant, K-VA-T Food Stores, Inc., d/b/a Food City is entitled to summary 
judgment as matter of law as to all of Plaintiff’s claims.                 
The undisputed material facts and applicable legal authority reflect that Food 
City is entitled to summary judgment as a matter of law as to all of Plaintiff’s claims.  
In accordance with Local Rule 56.1B, Food City has simultaneously filed its 
Statement of Facts as to which there is No Genuine Issue and Memorandum of Law.
