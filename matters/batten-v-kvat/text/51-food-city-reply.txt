Plaintiff relies on the non-binding decision of Hollingsworth v. O’Reilly Auto 
Stores, Inc. No. 4:13-cv-01623-KOB, 2015 U.S. LEXIS 10957 (N.D. Ala.2015) for 
the proposition that even if the Plaintiff was able to perform the essential functions 
of his job without accommodation, the employer may still be obligated to provide a 
reasonable accommodation to the employee.  
 
Plaintiff’s reliance on Hollingsworth is misplaced for two reasons. First, the 
precedent in the Eleventh Circuit on this issue is set forth recently in a 2022 decision 
from the Court of Appeals, Edwards v. WellStar Med. Grp., LLC, 2022 U.S. App. 
LEXIS 21036, 2022 WL 3012297, wherein the Court of Appeals affirmed summary 
judgment to the employer on plaintiff’s ADA claim because the plaintiff failed to

establish that she needed an accommodation to perform the essential job duties. The 
Court of Appeals recognized as follows:  
…a plaintiff must show that the accommodation is necessary – in that 
the accommodation “makes it possible” for her to do her job….…If an 
employee does not require an accommodation to perform her essential 
job functions, then the employer is under no obligation to make an 
accommodation, even if the employee requires an accommodation that 
is reasonable and could be easily provided.  
 
Edwards v. WellStar Med. Grp., LLC, 2022 U.S. App. LEXIS 21036, *16-17, 
citing LaChance v. Duffy’s Draft House, Inc, 146 F.3d 832, 835 (11th Cir. 1998) 
and D’Onofrio v. Costco Wholesale, Corp. 964 F.3d 1014, 1022 (11th Cir. 2020), 
cert. denied, 2021 U.S. LEXIS 826, 141 S. Ct. 1435, 209 L. Ed. 2d 155 (emphasis 
in original).  
    
 
The second reason Plaintiff’s reliance on Hollingsworth is misplaced is based 
on the facts, which are easily distinguishable from the facts before this Court. In 
Hollingsworth, the plaintiff had complained to his employer that he was having 
difficulty lifting items over 60 pounds because the other employees on his team were 
not assisting him, which was a condition stated in his job description. The plaintiff 
testified he had complained to his employer about this issue. Hollingsworth, 2015 
U.S. LEXIS 10957, *27-28.  
 
Plaintiff herein never reported any falls or performance issues to Food City 
during the two years he performed his job without incident. It was only when he 
obtained his service dog that he claimed to have difficulties performing his job. 
Contrary to Plaintiff’s assertion that D’Onofrio is “in conflict with other case law in 
this jurisdiction” (Doc. 42, p. 17), the Plaintiff only cites to cases from outside the

C.  
FOOD CITY DID NOT VIOLATE THE ADA BASED ON THE 
 
DENIAL OF PLAINTIFF’S REQUEST FOR A SERVICE DOG 
 
 
The ADA does not impose liability on an employer for failure to provide the 
accommodation particularly desired by an employee. Rather, the ADA only requires 
an employer, such as Food City, to engage in the interactive process and provide a 
reasonable accommodation.  
 
As noted in the recent case of Owens v. Governor's Office of Student 
Achievement, 52 F. 4th 327, 2022 LEXIS 31087 (11th Circuit, November 9, 2022), 
cert. denied, 2023 LEXIS 2048 (U.S., May 15, 2023):  
We have made clear that “an employer is not required to accommodate 
an employee in any manner that the employee desires - or even provide 
that employee’s preferred accommodation.” D’Onofrio, 964 F.3rd  1014, 
1022, cert. denied, 141 S. Ct. 1435, 209 L.Ed. 2d 155 (2021). Therefore, 
when an employee triggers an employer’s accommodation duties, the 
employer must expend time and expense to explore the universe of 
reasonable accommodations, identify one that is mutually agreeable to 
the parties, and implement it. 
 
Owens v. Georgia, Governor's Office of Student Achievement, 52 F.4th 1327, 
1335 (11th Cir. 2022).

In this case, the Plaintiff has admitted that he would not consider any 
accommodation that did not include his service dog. By not considering any other 
options, the Plaintiff failed to engage in the interactive process and precluded Food 
City’s ability to engage in the interactive process and prevented the consideration 
of “the universe of reasonable accommodations.” Owens v. Georgia, Governor's 
Office of Student Achievement, 52 F.4th 1327, 1335. 
 
D.  
PLAINTIFF HAS NO EVIDENCE OF RETALIATION  
 
 
To establish a prima facie case of retaliation, a plaintiff must show: (1) 
statutorily protected expression; (2) adverse employment action; and (3) a causal 
link between the protected expression and the adverse action. Stewart v. Happy 
Herman's Cheshire Bridge, 117 F.3d 1278, 1287 (11th Cir. 1997). Plaintiff has failed 
to state any facts supporting his allegation of a causal link between the Plaintiff’s 
statutorily protected conduct and the eventual separation of the Plaintiff from 
employment. Without factual support for his allegation of retaliation, Food City is 
entitled to summary judgment on the issue of alleged retaliation under the ADA.
